Source: Reddit thread - "What's the real monthly cost of off-campus housing?"

Question:
Rent is one number but what does it ACTUALLY cost per month once everything is added in? Trying to budget.

Student responses:

- Rent is maybe 70% of the real number. My "$800 room" was closer to $1000/month after utilities, internet, and renters insurance. Always ask what's included before comparing places.

- Utilities that are usually NOT included: electricity, gas/heat, water/sewer, garbage, internet. Some places bundle water and garbage into rent, almost none include electricity. Ask specifically.

- Heat is the budget killer in winter. My electric bill went from $40 in October to $130 in January. If a place has electric baseboard heat, expect high winter bills. Gas heat is usually cheaper.

- Split utilities fairly with roommates from day one. We put one person's name on each bill and Venmo them our share monthly. Decide who's responsible for what before move-in or it gets messy.

- Renters insurance is cheap (around $10-15/month) and a lot of landlords now require it. It covers your stuff if there's a fire or theft. Worth it even when not required.

- Internet is around $50-70/month for a household. Split between roommates it's small, but solo it adds up. Some complexes include it, which is a real perk.

- Don't forget one-time move-in costs: security deposit (often a full month's rent), first and sometimes last month upfront, application fees, and any move-in fee. Budget for a big lump sum at the start, not just the monthly rent.

- My rough rule: take the advertised rent and add 25-30% to estimate the true monthly cost with utilities and insurance.
