Source: Reddit thread - "Almost got scammed looking for student housing - PSA"

Question:
PSA for anyone apartment hunting. Here are the scams I ran into and how to spot them.

Student responses:

- The classic scam: a listing with great photos and below-market rent. The "landlord" says they're out of town and can't show it, but if you send a deposit they'll mail you the keys. NEVER send money for a place you haven't seen in person. If you can't tour it, walk away.

- Reverse image search the listing photos. Scammers steal photos from real listings on other sites. If the same photos show up on a Zillow listing in another city, it's fake.

- Be suspicious of anyone who only communicates by text or email and refuses a phone or video call. Real landlords and property managers will talk to you.

- Never pay a deposit in gift cards, wire transfer, Venmo, Zelle, or crypto. Those are untraceable and unrecoverable. Legitimate deposits go by check or through a payment portal with a paper trail.

- If the rent is dramatically below everything else near campus, that's the bait. Scammers price low on purpose to create urgency.

- Verify the person actually owns or manages the property. Look up the address on the county assessor site to see the real owner's name. If it doesn't match who you're talking to, ask questions.

- "I have many applicants, send the deposit now to hold it" is a pressure tactic. A real landlord will let you see the place and read the lease before you pay.

- When in doubt, ask the campus housing office. They sometimes keep lists of known scams targeting students and can tell you if a "landlord" has been reported.
