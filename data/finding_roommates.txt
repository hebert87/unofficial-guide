Source: Reddit thread - "How do you find roommates near campus?"

Question:
I'm transferring in and don't know anyone. How do people find roommates for off-campus housing?

Student responses:

- The Green River housing Facebook group is the main place. People post "looking for roommate" and "room available" constantly. Check it daily because good rooms go fast.

- Be careful with roommates you meet online. Always video call first, and try to meet in person before signing anything together. I moved in with a stranger who turned out to never pay rent on time and I was stuck covering it because we were on a joint lease.

- Joint lease vs. individual lease matters a LOT. On a joint lease, if your roommate doesn't pay, you're legally responsible for their share. On an individual lease (by-the-room), you're only responsible for yourself. By-the-room is safer but usually more expensive.

- Set expectations BEFORE moving in: cleaning schedule, guests, quiet hours, how bills get split. Write it down. The fights are never about the big stuff, they're about dishes and the thermostat.

- Splitting a house 5-6 ways is the cheapest option by far. I pay $770/month in a 7-person house. The tradeoff is one bathroom for several people and a lot of personalities under one roof.

- International and first-year students: the school sometimes has a roommate matching service. Ask the housing office even if you want to live off campus, they sometimes know of openings.

- Ask a potential roommate about their schedule and habits, not just whether they're "chill." A night-shift worker and an 8am-class person can clash hard.
