Source: Reddit thread - "What do you do about housing over the summer?"

Question:
My lease is 12 months but I go home for the summer. What are my options so I'm not paying for an empty room?

Student responses:

- Most student leases are 12 months specifically so you can't easily leave for summer. They know what they're doing. Always check whether your lease is 9-month (academic year) or 12-month before signing.

- Subletting is the usual move. You find someone to take over your room for the summer and pay the rent. BUT check your lease first, many landlords prohibit subletting or require written approval. Subletting against your lease can get you evicted.

- I sublet to a summer intern through the housing Facebook group. Get it in writing, collect a deposit from the subletter, and do a move-in inspection with them just like a landlord would. If they trash the place, you're still on the hook with your landlord.

- Some complexes offer summer-only leases at a discount because they have empty units. If you're staying for summer classes, ask about short-term rates.

- If you can't sublet, sometimes a roommate will cover your room for storage and you just keep paying a reduced share. Negotiate this before you all sign.

- Storage is the other summer problem. A small storage unit is cheaper than paying full rent on an empty room if you can't sublet, but moving everything twice is a pain.

- Don't sign a 12-month lease assuming you'll "figure out summer later." Figure it out before you sign, or you'll pay for months you're not there.
